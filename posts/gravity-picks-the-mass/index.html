Gravity Picks the Mass: A Program for Asymptotically Safe Leptons

November 10, 2025 · Leo Schneider

In my last few posts I tried to hide the electron’s mass inside the wavefunction and even wondered whether probability density should carry its own mass scale. Those thought experiments were fun, but they kept running into two walls: Born’s rule still works obnoxiously well, and mass labels a Poincaré representation, not a position eigenstate. The only way to make mass emerge is to find a UV principle that refuses to let Yukawa couplings float freely.

Enter Option 3 from the previous discussion: asymptotic safety. If quantum gravity forces the Standard Model couplings toward an interacting ultraviolet (UV) fixed point, only a handful of “relevant” combinations survive. Everything else—Yukawas included—gets nailed to discrete values fixed by that UV critical surface. The rest of this article sketches a concrete research program to see whether the three charged-lepton masses could be such outputs.

Why gravity cares about Yukawas#

Functional renormalization group (FRG) studies start from an average effective action \(\Gamma_k\) that flows with the scale \(k\) according to the Wetterich equation $$ \partial_t \Gamma_k = \frac{1}{2}\ \mathrm{STr}\Big[ (\Gamma_k^{(2)} + R_k)^{-1} \partial_t R_k \Big]\ \quad t \equiv \ln k, $$ and truncate \(\Gamma_k\) to the fields of interest. For gravity + one scalar + one Dirac fermion you can take $$ \Gamma_k = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \Big[ -\frac{1}{16\pi G_k}(R - 2\Lambda_k) + Z_{\psi,k},\bar\psi i\not{!D} \psi + \tfrac{1}{2} Z_{\phi,k} (\nabla \phi)^2 - U_k(\phi) - y_k , \phi \bar\psi \psi \Big]. $$ The beta function for the Yukawa coupling schematically reads $$ \beta_{y} = \partial_t y_k = y_k \Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{\phi} + \eta_{\psi}\Big) + \frac{c_1}{16\pi^2} y_k^3 + c_2 g_k y_k + \cdots, $$ where \(g_k = G_k k^2\) and the anomalous dimensions \(\eta\) encode matter + gravity fluctuations. The last term proportional to \(g_k\) is crucial: gravitational anti-screening can offset the usual positive \(y^3\) term, generating an interacting fixed point \(y_* \neq 0\). Eichhorn and Held showed this explicitly for Dirac + scalar systems, finding a finite set of relevant directions once gravity is included—exactly the discretization lever we need. 1

Once you enlarge the truncation to something Standard Model–like (e.g., add \(B!- B\) gauge fields, multiple fermions, and the Higgs doublet), the same story persists: the Yukawa matrix is pulled toward a narrow subset compatible with the UV fixed point, and neutrino masses can even emerge without put-in-by-hand small parameters. Pawlowski, Reichert, and Smids demonstrated this for a gauged \(B!- L\) extension, where asymptotic safety dynamically suppresses neutrino Yukawas to the observed scale. 2

More recently, next-to-leading-order truncations that include Pauli operators in QED-like sectors show that gravity selects only a few viable combinations of the electron mass, charge, and anomalous magnetic moment. That analysis tightened the count of relevant directions to three, hinting that “three masses” is not an unreasonable dream. 3 Complementary work on coupled gravity–tensor theories found that interacting fixed points for Yukawa systems only exist when discrete relations among matter quartic couplings hold, reinforcing the idea that topology-plus-gravity can carve out a lattice of admissible masses. 4

The upshot: gravity already quantizes coupling space by reducing the dimensionality of the UV critical surface. All we have to do is see whether \(m_e, m_{\mu}, m_{\tau}\) fit onto that surface.

A concrete four-stage program#

Here is the experiment I want to run on paper (and, inevitably, in Python notebooks):

1. Reproduce the benchmark fixed point#

Re-derive the Einstein–Hilbert + scalar + Dirac truncation of Eichhorn & Held using the regulator and gauge choices they employed. The deliverables are:

  • Numerical values for \(g_*\), \(\lambda_*\), and \(y_*\).
  • The stability matrix \(M_{ij} = \partial \beta_i / \partial g_j\) evaluated at the fixed point, plus its eigenvalues.
  • A check that only \(N_r\) directions (ideally \(N_r = 2\) or 3) are relevant. This stage ensures our code reproduces published gravity-induced Yukawa anti-screening before we add complexity. 1

2. Upgrade to three charged leptons + one Higgs#

Introduce three Dirac flavors \(\psi_{e,\mu,\tau}\) coupled to a single real scalar (standing in for the Higgs). The Yukawa matrix \(Y\) is now \(3\times 1\). Compute the enlarged stability matrix and look for how many linear combinations of \(Y\) remain relevant. In the ideal outcome exactly three survive, so each physical charged lepton corresponds to one relevant direction of the UV critical surface. To keep things manageable:

  • Treat gauge couplings as background (fixed to their measured values at the Planck scale) in a first pass.
  • Use symmetric and antisymmetric combinations of Yukawas to diagonalize the flow analytically as far as possible.
  • Track the anomalous dimensions \(\eta_{\psi_i}\); gravity can make them nondegenerate, splitting the masses even without extra symmetry assumptions. 5

3. Run down to the electroweak scale#

Pick UV initial conditions lying on the critical surface from Stage 2 and integrate the coupled beta functions down to \(k \sim 100\) GeV. Use the full one-loop Standard Model RGEs for \(Y_e, Y_\mu, Y_\tau\) once gravity decouples near \(k \sim 10^{18}\) GeV. The questions to answer:

  • Do any trajectories land on the observed masses \((0.511, 105.7, 1776.9)\) MeV simultaneously?
  • If not, which additional interactions (e.g., \(B!- L\) gauge sector, higher-derivative gravity terms) need to be activated to gain one more relevant direction? The Pawlowski–Reichert neutrino study already provides a template for this matching procedure. 2

4. Identify observables and falsifiers#

Even if the charged-lepton masses can be fitted, asymptotic safety makes collateral predictions:

  • Fixed-point values for dipole operators constrain \((g-2)_e\) and \((g-2)_\mu\). Compare against the Pauli-term analysis to see whether the same critical surface survives. 3
  • The number of relevant directions dictates how many free parameters the low-energy theory retains. If you need more than three to hit the data, the elegant “gravity picks the mass” story fails.
  • Cosmology: matching onto reheating or leptogenesis scenarios will tie the UV yukawas to the baryon asymmetry. The tensor-field fixed points from the recent PRD study imply only certain quartic couplings remain, so anything outside that window would immediately falsify the setup. 4

Deliverables for the blog series#

  1. Notebook 1 – Reproduce the benchmark beta functions, compute the stability matrix, and visualize the RG flow in \((g, \lambda, y)\) space.
  2. Notebook 2 – Extend to three Yukawas, perform an eigenbasis decomposition, and identify the gravity-protected combinations.
  3. Notebook 3 – Integrate UV → IR, compare against the measured lepton spectrum, and map out how sensitive the result is to Planck-scale threshold choices.
  4. Postmortem article – If gravity fails to quantize the masses cleanly, document exactly why (too many relevant directions? wrong hierarchy?) so we can pivot to extra symmetries or flux backgrounds.

In other words, rather than stuffing mass into the wavefunction, we let gravity squeeze it out of the RG. Option 3 is no longer just a philosophical preference; it’s a to-do list.
