The Cosmic Double-Slit: Could Quantum Gravity Only Appear at Galactic Scales?

November 9, 2025 · Leo Schneider

In my article on punctual wavefunctions, I argued that classical mechanics emerges from quantum mechanics when wavefunctions become sufficiently localized. The WKB approximation shows that such wavefunctions follow classical trajectories encoded in their quantum phase. This seems to explain why baseballs behave classically while electrons exhibit quantum interference.

But there’s a subtlety I didn’t fully address. Even a baseball—with its highly localized, “punctual” wavefunction—still has a de Broglie wavelength. In principle, it should still create an interference pattern in a double-slit experiment. The quantum behavior doesn’t disappear; it just becomes invisible at laboratory scales.

This observation led me to a question that, at first, seemed merely curious but has grown increasingly provocative: What if the same thing happens with gravity?

Why We Don’t See Baseball Interference#

Consider the standard double-slit interference pattern. The spacing between bright fringes on a screen is given by: $$\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$$

where:

  • \(\lambda = h/p\) is the de Broglie wavelength
  • \(L\) is the distance from slits to screen
  • \(d\) is the separation between slits

For an electron moving at \(10^6\) m/s, the de Broglie wavelength is \(\lambda \sim 10^{-9}\) m. With typical laboratory dimensions (\(d \sim 1\) mm, \(L \sim 1\) m), the fringe spacing is \(\Delta y \sim 10^{-6}\) m = 1 micron. Easily visible under a microscope.

Now consider a baseball. Mass \(m \sim 0.1\) kg, velocity \(v \sim 10\) m/s: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{0.1 \times 10} \approx 6.6 \times 10^{-34} \text{ m}$$

With the same slit separation (\(d \sim 1\) mm), how far away must the screen be to see even one fringe with spacing \(\Delta y \sim 1\) mm? $$L = \frac{\Delta y \cdot d}{\lambda} = \frac{10^{-3} \times 10^{-3}}{6.6 \times 10^{-34}} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{27} \text{ m}$$

That’s about 160 million light-years—comparable to the distance to the Virgo Cluster. The interference pattern exists. Your laboratory is simply too small to contain it.

The General Principle#

Quantum interference doesn’t vanish for macroscopic objects, it becomes diluted over enormous length scales. The characteristic distance at which quantum effects become observable is: $$L_{\text{quantum}} \sim \frac{d}{\lambda} \times \Delta y_{\text{min}} = \frac{pd}{\hbar} \times \Delta y_{\text{min}}$$

For massive objects with large momentum \(p\), this length scale becomes astronomical.

The quantum-classical boundary is not a sharp line; it’s a smooth transition controlled by the ratio \(L/L_{\text{quantum}}\).

This raises a question: Could other phenomena exhibit similar scale-dependent quantum effects, appearing classical at small distances but revealing quantum interference at cosmological scales?

The MOND Mystery#

In 1933, Fritz Zwicky observed that galaxies in the Coma Cluster were moving far too fast to be bound by the visible matter’s gravitational pull. He proposed that some “dunkle Materie” (dark matter) must exist to provide additional gravity. By the 1970s, Vera Rubin’s observations of galactic rotation curves confirmed that something was deeply wrong with our understanding of gravity at galactic scales.

Stars at the edge of spiral galaxies orbit at constant velocity rather than slowing down as Newtonian gravity predicts. The conventional explanation is dark matter: an invisible substance that outweighs visible matter by a factor of 5-6, distributed in enormous halos around galaxies.

But in 1983, Mordehai Milgrom proposed a radical alternative. Instead of adding dark matter, modify Newton’s law of gravity. His Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) posits that at very low accelerations, gravity behaves differently: $$a = \begin{cases} a_N & \text{if } a_N \gg a_0 \\ \sqrt{a_N a_0} & \text{if } a_N \ll a_0 \end{cases}$$

where \(a_N = GM/r^2\) is the Newtonian acceleration and \(a_0\) is a fundamental acceleration scale.

Remarkably, with a single parameter: $$a_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m/s}^2$$

MOND fits galactic rotation curves across hundreds of galaxies with no additional free parameters. The theory is empirically successful to a degree that seems too good to be coincidence.

The Cosmological Connection#

What makes \(a_0\) truly mysterious is its value. It’s not arbitrary; it’s suspiciously close to a cosmological scale: $$a_0 \approx c H_0$$

where \(c\) is the speed of light and \(H_0 \approx 70\) km/s/Mpc \(\approx 2.3 \times 10^{-18}\) s⁻¹ is the Hubble constant.

Numerically: $$c H_0 = (3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}) \times (2.3 \times 10^{-18} \text{ s}^{-1}) \approx 7 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m/s}^2$$

This is within a factor of 6 of the observed \(a_0\). Given that cosmological parameters span 120 orders of magnitude, this agreement is striking.

Why should a phenomenon occurring in individual galaxies be connected to the expansion rate of the entire universe?

Quantum Interference at Cosmological Scales?#

What if gravity, like particle interference, exhibits quantum effects that become observable only at sufficiently large distances?

In the double-slit experiment, we saw that:

  • Short distances (\(L \ll L_{\text{quantum}}\)): Classical behavior, well-defined trajectories
  • Long distances (\(L \gg L_{\text{quantum}}\)): Quantum interference, modified dynamics

Could the same apply to spacetime geometry itself?

  • Short distances (Solar System scales): Classical general relativity
  • Long distances (galactic scales): Quantum corrections become significant

The acceleration scale \(a_0\) might be the gravitational analog of the interference scale \(\Delta y\) in the double-slit experiment. We will explore several possible explanations for this coincidence.

Attempt 1: The Cosmic Horizon as a Boundary Condition#

In quantum mechanics, boundary conditions determine interference patterns. The walls of a box quantize energy levels, the slits in the double slit experiment determine fringe spacing.

For gravity, what is the relevant boundary? It is the cosmic horizon.

The Hubble horizon is the distance \(R_H\) beyond which objects recede faster than light due to cosmic expansion: $$R_H = \frac{c}{H_0} \approx 1.3 \times 10^{26} \text{ m} \approx 14 \text{ billion light-years}$$

This defines a causal boundary. Information from beyond \(R_H\) has not yet reached us; information from inside cannot reach beyond.

Now consider a test mass at distance \(r\) from a central gravitating mass \(M\). The Newtonian acceleration is: $$a_N = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

But suppose that gravity, like a quantum wavefunction, must satisfy boundary conditions at the cosmic horizon. The gravitational “wavefunction” must vanish (or have a node) at \(r = R_H\).

By analogy with the particle in a box, where energy quantization arises from \(\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0\), we might expect gravitational quantization with characteristic scale: $$\Delta a \sim \frac{GM}{R_H^2}$$

But this gives the wrong scale. For a galaxy with \(M \sim 10^{11} M_{\odot} \sim 2 \times 10^{41}\) kg: $$\Delta a \sim \frac{(6.7 \times 10^{-11})(2 \times 10^{41})}{(1.3 \times 10^{26})^2} \sim 10^{-20} \text{ m/s}^2$$

Far too small. But wait—this calculation assumes the mass \(M\) is fixed. What if we instead consider the characteristic acceleration produced by the vacuum energy at the horizon?

The vacuum energy density is1: $$\rho_\Lambda = \frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G}$$

The gravitational effect of vacuum energy over scale \(R_H\) produces acceleration: $$a_{\text{vacuum}} \sim \frac{G \rho_\Lambda R_H^3}{R_H^2} = G \rho_\Lambda R_H = \frac{3H_0^2 R_H}{8\pi} = \frac{3H_0 c}{8\pi}$$

Using \(H_0 \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-18}\) s⁻¹: $$a_{\text{vacuum}} \sim \frac{3 \times 2.3 \times 10^{-18} \times 3 \times 10^8}{8\pi} \sim 8 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m/s}^2$$

This is exactly the right order of magnitude!

The characteristic acceleration scale in a universe with cosmological constant \(\Lambda\) and Hubble rate \(H_0\) is: $$\boxed{a_0 \sim cH_0 \sim \sqrt{\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}}}$$

This is not a fitted parameter—it’s a prediction from cosmology.

Attempt 2: Holographic Entropy and Information Bounds#

The holographic principle, motivated by black hole thermodynamics, states that the maximum entropy in a region of space is proportional to its boundary area, not its volume: $$S_{\text{max}} = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4G\hbar}$$

where \(A\) is the area in Planck units.

Erik Verlinde has argued that gravity is not a fundamental force but an emergent thermodynamic phenomenon arising from quantum entanglement entropy. In his 2016 paper “Emergent Gravity and the Dark Universe,” he attempts to derive MOND-like behavior from first principles.

The key insight: the number of degrees of freedom on the cosmic horizon is finite and set by the de Sitter entropy: $$N_{\text{dS}} = \frac{k_B c^3}{G\hbar H_0}$$

These degrees of freedom store information about matter in the universe. When you move a mass \(M\) through space, you change the entanglement structure, which requires “displacing” information on the horizon.

The acceleration required to displace one bit of information over the horizon is: $$a_{\text{info}} \sim \frac{c^2}{R_H} = cH_0$$

Verlinde argues that in the low-acceleration regime (\(a \ll cH_0\)), the elastic response of spacetime to mass displacement gives: $$a_{\text{apparent}} = \sqrt{a_N \cdot cH_0}$$

This is precisely the MOND formula with \(a_0 = cH_0\)!

The acceleration scale emerges from information theory and holography.

Attempt 3: Quantum Coherence Length for Gravitons#

Let’s approach this from a different angle: quantum interference.

In the double-slit experiment, particles create interference patterns because their wavefunctions remain coherent across both slits. The coherence length is roughly: $$\ell_{\text{coh}} \sim \frac{\lambda \cdot L}{d}$$

For massive particles, \(\lambda = h/p\) is tiny, so coherence lengths are small at laboratory scales.

But what about gravitons—the hypothetical quantum particles that mediate gravity?

In quantum field theory, the coherence length of a massless boson is related to the uncertainty in its energy: $$\ell_{\text{coh}} \sim \frac{\hbar c}{\Delta E}$$

For gravitons emitted by a massive object with gravitational binding energy \(E_g \sim GM^2/R\), the energy uncertainty over cosmological time \(t \sim H_0^{-1}\) is: $$\Delta E \sim \frac{E_g}{H_0^{-1}} \sim \frac{GM^2 H_0}{R}$$

Wait, this is getting messy. Let me try a cleaner approach.

Time-energy uncertainty: For processes occurring over cosmological time \(\Delta t \sim H_0^{-1}\), the energy uncertainty is: $$\Delta E \sim \frac{\hbar}{H_0^{-1}} = \hbar H_0$$

The coherence length for gravitons with this energy uncertainty is: $$\ell_{\text{coh}} \sim \frac{c}{\Delta \omega} \sim \frac{c}{H_0} = R_H$$

Gravitons have coherence lengths comparable to the cosmic horizon!

This means gravity at distances \(r \sim R_H\) exhibits quantum interference, while at \(r \ll R_H\) it appears classical.

The transition occurs when the gravitational acceleration becomes comparable to: $$a_0 \sim \frac{c^2}{R_H} = cH_0$$

Attempt 4: Uncertainty Principle for Spacetime#

There’s another way to arrive at \(a_0\) using Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle applied to gravitational fields.

Consider a mass distribution with uncertainty \(\Delta M\) spread over region of size \(R\). The gravitational field has uncertainty: $$\Delta g \sim \frac{G \Delta M}{R^2}$$

The time uncertainty for measuring this field (limited by light travel) is: $$\Delta t \sim \frac{R}{c}$$

By dimensional analysis, we can form an “acceleration uncertainty”: $$\Delta a \sim \frac{\Delta g}{\Delta t} \sim \frac{G \Delta M c}{R^3}$$

Now, for quantum gravity to be relevant, the Schwarzschild radius \(r_s = 2GM/c^2\) should be comparable to the Compton wavelength \(\lambda_C = \hbar/(Mc)\): $$\frac{GM}{c^2} \sim \frac{\hbar}{Mc}$$ $$M \sim \sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{G}} = m_{\text{Planck}}$$

For masses \(M \ll m_{\text{Planck}}\), quantum effects dominate. For \(M \gg m_{\text{Planck}}\), classical gravity dominates.

But at cosmological scales, there’s another mass scale: the total mass within the Hubble horizon: $$M_H \sim \rho_{\text{crit}} \cdot R_H^3 \sim \frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G} \cdot \left(\frac{c}{H_0}\right)^3 = \frac{3c^3}{8\pi G H_0}$$

The characteristic acceleration at the horizon is: $$a_H \sim \frac{GM_H}{R_H^2} = \frac{G}{R_H^2} \cdot \frac{3c^3}{8\pi G H_0} = \frac{3c^3}{8\pi R_H^2 H_0} = \frac{3cH_0}{8\pi}$$

Once again, we get: $$\boxed{a_0 \sim cH_0}$$

Part IV: Making It Precise#

Let me synthesize these arguments into a single coherent derivation.

The Derivation#

Postulate 1: Spacetime has a finite information capacity set by the holographic principle. The maximum number of degrees of freedom within the cosmic horizon is: $$N = \frac{A_H}{4\ell_P^2} = \frac{4\pi R_H^2}{4\ell_P^2} = \frac{\pi c^3}{\hbar G H_0^2}$$

where \(\ell_P = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}\) is the Planck length and \(R_H = c/H_0\) is the Hubble radius.

Postulate 2: These degrees of freedom are maximally entangled. Moving a mass \(M\) through space changes the entanglement structure, requiring energy: $$E_{\text{ent}} \sim \frac{M c^2}{N}$$

per degree of freedom displaced.

Postulate 3: The force required to displace mass \(M\) over distance \(r\) in time \(t \sim r/c\) is: $$F \sim \frac{E_{\text{ent}}}{r} \sim \frac{M c^2}{N r} = \frac{M c^2 \hbar G H_0^2}{\pi c^3 r}$$

Wait, this isn’t working cleanly. Let me try Verlinde’s approach more carefully.

Verlinde’s Emergent Gravity (Simplified)#

In Verlinde’s framework, gravity emerges from entropy gradients. The key equation is: $$F = T \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta x}$$

where \(T\) is an effective temperature and \(S\) is entropy.

For a mass \(M\) at distance \(r\) from a central mass \(M_0\), the entropy associated with the holographic screen is: $$S = \frac{2\pi k_B M c r}{\hbar}$$

The entropy gradient gives: $$F = T \frac{\partial S}{\partial r} = T \frac{2\pi k_B M c}{\hbar}$$

The temperature is related to acceleration by the Unruh effect: $$T = \frac{\hbar a}{2\pi k_B c}$$

Combining: $$F = \frac{\hbar a}{2\pi k_B c} \cdot \frac{2\pi k_B M c}{\hbar} = Ma$$

This is just Newton’s second law—it had better work! The question is: what determines \(a\)?

In the classical (high acceleration) regime, the entropy is large and “stiff”—it responds linearly to displacement. But in the quantum (low acceleration) regime, the entropy is limited by the finite number of degrees of freedom on the cosmic horizon.

Verlinde argues that when \(a \lesssim cH_0\), you enter a regime where the “elastic response” of the horizon modifies the effective force: $$a_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{a_N \cdot cH_0}$$

The detailed derivation requires the full apparatus of holographic screens and de Sitter thermodynamics, which I’m eliding here. But the key point is:

The acceleration scale \(a_0 = cH_0\) emerges from the finite information capacity of the cosmic horizon.

A Cleaner Derivation: Dimensional Analysis#

Let’s use dimensional analysis to see what acceleration scale must appear in a theory of quantum gravity with cosmology.

We have four fundamental constants:

  • \(\hbar\) (quantum mechanics)
  • \(c\) (relativity)
  • \(G\) (gravity)
  • \(H_0\) (cosmology)

From these, we can form several quantities with dimensions of acceleration:

  1. Planck acceleration: \(a_P = c^7/(G\hbar) \sim 10^{52}\) m/s² (absurdly large)
  2. Cosmological acceleration: \(a_H = cH_0 \sim 10^{-10}\) m/s² (right scale!)
  3. Gravitational coupling: \(G\rho c\) where \(\rho\) is a density (needs mass scale)

The only dimensionally correct acceleration scale that emerges naturally from fundamental constants without introducing additional mass scales is: $$\boxed{a_0 = \alpha \cdot cH_0}$$

where \(\alpha\) is a dimensionless constant of order unity.

Different theoretical approaches (holographic principle, vacuum energy, information theory) give \(\alpha\) ranging from \(1/(8\pi) \approx 0.04\) to \(1\), which matches the observed \(a_0 \approx (0.2\text{–}1) \times cH_0\).

This is as close as we can get to a first-principles derivation without a complete theory of quantum gravity.

Part V: Predictions and Tests#

If MOND-like behavior arises from quantum interference at cosmological scales, we can make predictions:

Prediction 1: \(a_0\) Should Evolve with Cosmic Time#

If \(a_0 \propto H(z)\) where \(H(z)\) is the Hubble parameter at redshift \(z\), then: $$a_0(z) = a_0(0) \times \frac{H(z)}{H_0}$$

In the early universe, \(H\) was much larger, so \(a_0\) was larger. MOND effects would have been much weaker.

Test: Measure rotation curves of galaxies at different redshifts. Check if the apparent dark matter content evolves with \(z\).

Status: Current data is ambiguous. High-redshift galaxies are harder to measure, but there are hints that rotation curves were different in the early universe.

Prediction 2: Connection to Dark Energy#

If \(a_0 \sim \sqrt{\Lambda c^2}\) where \(\Lambda\) is the cosmological constant, then the same physics that explains MOND should also explain dark energy.

This is Verlinde’s claim: both dark matter (MOND) and dark energy arise from holographic quantum effects.

Test: Measure \(\Lambda\) and \(a_0\) independently; check if \(a_0^2 \propto \Lambda\).

Status: The proportionality roughly holds (within a factor of a few), but more precision is needed.

Prediction 3: Deviations from MOND at Larger Scales#

If quantum interference has a finite coherence length \(\sim R_H\), then at scales \(r \gtrsim R_H\), even the MOND formula should break down.

Test: Cosmological simulations with MOND vs. dark matter. Check structure formation at the largest scales.

Status: MOND has difficulty explaining:

  • Bullet Cluster (where dark matter is “separated” from baryons)
  • CMB power spectrum (needs more dark matter than MOND provides)
  • Large-scale structure (needs cold dark matter simulations)

This suggests that MOND alone is insufficient—perhaps both modified gravity and some dark matter are needed.

Prediction 4: Gravitational Wave Propagation#

If gravity exhibits quantum interference at large scales, gravitational waves might experience “dispersion” similar to light in a medium.

The group velocity might depend on frequency: $$v_g(f) = c\left(1 - \frac{f_0^2}{f^2}\right)$$

where \(f_0 \sim H_0 \sim 10^{-18}\) Hz.

Test: Multi-messenger astronomy. Compare arrival times of gravitational waves and light from the same event (like neutron star mergers).

Status: GW170817 (neutron star merger) showed that gravitational waves travel at the speed of light to within 1 part in \(10^{15}\). This strongly constrains any modified gravity theories.

However, this test is at frequencies \(f \sim 100\) Hz, far above \(f_0 \sim 10^{-18}\) Hz. Dispersion effects would be negligible.

Part VI: Objections and Open Questions#

Objection 1: The Bullet Cluster#

The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56) is two galaxy clusters that collided. Gravitational lensing shows that the mass distribution is offset from the gas (baryonic matter).

MOND cannot explain this: the gravitational lensing should follow the baryons, but it doesn’t. Dark matter seems necessary.

Possible response: Perhaps both MOND-like effects and some dark matter exist? The universe might be more complex than either pure MOND or pure dark matter.

Objection 2: The CMB Power Spectrum#

The cosmic microwave background has an acoustic peak structure that is exquisitely fit by \(\Lambda\)CDM (cold dark matter + cosmological constant).

MOND struggles to reproduce these peaks without adding extra dark matter.

Possible response: MOND was formulated for galactic dynamics, not cosmology. A complete theory of emergent gravity might have different behavior at recombination (\(z \sim 1100\)) when \(H(z)\) was much larger.

Objection 3: No Quantum Gravity Theory#

We have no confirmed theory of quantum gravity. String theory, loop quantum gravity, asymptotic safety—all are works in progress.

Without knowing how to quantize spacetime, deriving \(a_0\) from first principles is premature.

Response: True. But the dimensional argument is robust: \(cH_0\) is the only acceleration scale that emerges from \((\hbar, c, G, H_0)\). Even without a full theory, this dimensional analysis is suggestive.

Objection 4: Why Should Local Physics Depend on Cosmology?#

The rotation curve of the Milky Way shouldn’t “know” about the Hubble constant. This seems to violate locality.

Possible response: In holographic theories, there is no sharp distinction between “local” and “global.” Information about the universe is encoded on boundaries. Verlinde’s framework explicitly connects local gravitational effects to global holographic screens.

Alternatively: If gravitational coherence lengths are \(\sim R_H\), then all gravitational phenomena within the horizon are quantum-mechanically entangled. Local dynamics must depend on global boundary conditions.

This is strange, but quantum mechanics is already non-local (Bell’s theorem). Perhaps spacetime is too.

Conclusion: The Scale-Dependent Nature of Reality#

The double-slit experiment with macroscopic objects teaches us that quantum effects don’t disappear—they become diluted over enormous length scales. A baseball exhibits interference, but only on screens spanning galactic distances.

Could the same be true of spacetime itself? Classical general relativity might be the short-distance limit of a quantum theory that reveals itself only at cosmological scales.

The MOND acceleration scale \(a_0 \approx cH_0\) emerges naturally from:

  • The finite information capacity of the cosmic horizon (holographic principle)
  • The coherence length of gravitons over cosmological time (quantum interference)
  • The vacuum energy density of the universe (dark energy)
  • Dimensional analysis of quantum gravity with cosmology

We cannot yet claim to have derived \(a_0\) from first principles—that would require a complete theory of quantum gravity. But we can see the outline of such a derivation.

If this picture is correct, it suggests:

  • Dark matter and dark energy are not separate mysteries—they are two manifestations of quantum spacetime
  • Gravity is not a fundamental force but an emergent thermodynamic phenomenon
  • The universe’s large-scale structure is shaped by quantum interference at the cosmic horizon
  • Locality is an approximation that breaks down when gravity becomes weak

These ideas are speculative, and the experimental evidence is mixed. MOND works remarkably well for galaxies but struggles with clusters and cosmology. Emergent gravity is elegant but incomplete. Dark matter remains the simpler explanation for most observations.

Yet the coincidence \(a_0 \approx cH_0\) is too striking to ignore. It suggests that our understanding of gravity, quantum mechanics, and cosmology is incomplete. Somewhere in the intersection of these three domains lies a deeper truth about the nature of spacetime.

The cosmos may be performing a double-slit experiment on itself, with interference fringes spanning billions of light-years. We are only beginning to see the pattern.

Postscript: This article is highly speculative. The ideas presented—emergent gravity, holographic MOND, quantum coherence at cosmological scales—are active areas of research with no consensus. I have attempted to be rigorous in dimensional analysis and honest about what can and cannot be derived from first principles.
